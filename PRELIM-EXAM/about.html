About Philippine Red Cross

Our history, mission, vision, and humanitarian principles

Our History

The Philippine Red Cross was established in 1947, just after the Philippines gained independence. It was born from the ashes of World War II, when the country needed humanitarian assistance to rebuild communities and heal wounds.

As a member of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, we've grown to become the foremost humanitarian organization in the Philippines, with chapters in all 81 provinces and 1,494 cities and municipalities.

From responding to typhoons and earthquakes to providing blood services and health programs, we've been at the forefront of humanitarian action for over 75 years.

Our Mission

To provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable, inspired by our Fundamental Principles, and to empower communities to become more resilient to disasters and health emergencies.

Our Vision

To be the leading humanitarian organization in the Philippines, committed to building safe, healthy, and resilient communities through the power of humanity.

Our Values

Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, Independence, Voluntary Service, Unity, and Universality guide all our actions and decisions.

Our Fundamental Principles

The seven principles that guide all Red Cross and Red Crescent activities worldwide

1

Humanity

To prevent and alleviate human suffering wherever it may be found, to protect life and health, and to ensure respect for the human being.

2

Impartiality

To make no discrimination as to nationality, race, religious beliefs, class or political opinions, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress.

3

Neutrality

To not take sides in hostilities or engage in controversies of a political, racial, religious or ideological nature.

4

Independence

To maintain autonomy so we may always act according to our principles, while auxiliaries to the public authorities in humanitarian services.

5

Voluntary Service

To be a voluntary relief movement not prompted in any manner by desire for gain.

6

Unity

To have only one Red Cross or Red Crescent Society in any one country, open to all and carrying its humanitarian work throughout the territory.

7

Universality

The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement is worldwide, with equal status and equal responsibilities in helping each other.
